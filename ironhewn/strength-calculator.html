How your rank
is calculated.

The same math as the Ironhewn app, start to finish. Here it is in plain words.

1. Your set becomes an estimated 1RM

We average two well-known formulas, Epley and Brzycki, which bracket each other closely up to 10 reps (sets of 11–12 lean on Epley). A single counts as it is. For pull-ups, chin-ups and dips, the load is your bodyweight plus anything you added.

2. Five checkpoints, Beginner to Elite

Standards are set for a reference lifter (a 176 lb / 80 kg man or a 132 lb / 60 kg woman) and placed near the 5th, 20th, 50th, 80th and 95th percentile of people who lift. Your level is where your 1RM falls between them.

3. Scaled to your bodyweight

Strength follows muscle cross-section, which grows more slowly than body mass. So the standards scale with bodyweight to the 0.67 power: a 240 lb lifter needs a bigger bench than a 160 lb lifter, but about 31% bigger, not 50%.

4. Adjusted for height and age

Taller lifters move the bar farther, so standards ease slightly with height (up to 7% either way). From age 35 you earn 1.2% credit a year, and lifters under 19 earn 2.5% a year: a 50-year-old’s standards sit about 15% lower.

Eleven metals,
three divisions each.

Your level maps onto a ladder from Rust to Starforged, and each metal splits into divisions III, II and I. Silver is the median lifter, Platinum is advanced, Orichalcum is elite, and Starforged is beyond.

Questions.

How is my strength rank calculated?

We turn your set into an estimated one-rep max (the average of the Epley and Brzycki formulas, the same math the Ironhewn app uses), then compare it with strength standards for your sex and bodyweight, adjusted for height and age. That gives a strength level from Beginner to Elite, which maps onto 11 metal ranks, from Rust to Starforged, with three divisions each.

Is this calculator accurate?

It’s a solid estimate, not a lab test. One-rep-max formulas are most reliable with sets of 1 to 5 reps and drift as the reps climb. The standards are approximations compiled from public strength-standard data, with each level set near the 5th, 20th, 50th, 80th and 95th percentile of people who lift. Use your rank for motivation and goal-setting, not as medical or coaching advice.

Why does bodyweight matter?

Bigger bodies carry more muscle, so they usually lift more, but not in proportion. Strength follows a muscle’s cross-section, which grows more slowly than body mass, so the standards scale with bodyweight to the 0.67 power instead of a simple ratio. Doubling your bodyweight raises the bar by about 59%, not 100%, which keeps the comparison fair for light and heavy lifters.

What’s a good bench press for my weight?

For a 180 lb man of average height and under 35, an intermediate bench (about the median lifter) is around 215 lb and an advanced bench is around 286 lb. For an 80 kg man that’s about 96 kg and 128 kg. For a 140 lb woman, intermediate is around 107 lb and advanced around 151 lb. Enter your own numbers above to see your exact standards. See the tables by bodyweight.

Do I need to test a true one-rep max?

No. Any hard set of 1 to 12 reps works, so there’s no need to grind out a risky single. Sets of 3 to 5 reps taken close to failure give the most reliable estimate. Count only reps with full range of motion: half reps inflate your rank.
